# The classifier prompt

Everything the model is told when it reads a paper, reproduced as it was sent for
classification. The system prompt in section 2
and the schema descriptions in section 3 are the model's complete instructions.

---

## 1. What the model receives

One request per paper (Batch API, GPT-5.6 Sol, effort medium, strict JSON schema):

- `arXiv:<id> — full paper source follows.`
- `[RENDERED]` — the paper's extracted TeX text, comments stripped. 
- `[COMMENT]` — the TeX comment stream (last ~12k chars), for the comment-only
  sub-series.

Scope: every 2023+ paper with ≥1 term-list hit gets this full-text read. 
---
## 2. The system prompt (verbatim)

> You read the full source of one arXiv mathematics paper and determine whether,
> and how, the authors disclose using AI tools in producing
> it. The input has the rendered text under [RENDERED] and TeX comments under
> [COMMENT]. You want to be as accurate as possible in determining the exact use cases for which AI use was disclosed. 
>
> The paper was selected by a keyword scan, so many papers here contain no
> disclosure: a matched term may be a person's name, a citation, or the paper's
> own subject matter. Getting those right matters as much as the disclosures.
>
> "AI" means any generative AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude, Codex, Gemini, or another LLM, or anything acknowledged as "AI" or as an "AI tool." Conventional software does not count as AI. If the paper itself trains or introduces its own AI model, this does not count. 
>
> Tag everything you find with the specific use case. Every tag requires an exact quoted passage from the paper justifying the inclusion of that tag. Every AI use-case acknowledged in a paper should be tagged separately. If AI use is acknowledged but no use-case is stated, use the tag "purpose_unstated." 
> Copy the exact characters, never paraphrase or splice with ellipses, and the
> stream it came from. No quote, no tag. Multi-tagging is normal: one
> acknowledgment often covers multiple different use cases. A list of several activities can receive one tag per activity. Judge only what the text states:
> do not infer undisclosed use from style, and do not upgrade a hedged statement.
>
> The tags are supposed to disambiguate between different use cases for AI, such as formalization, writing/drafting, or research use. Each of the tags main_results_generated, specific_result_generated, statement_generated, substantial_proof_contributions, proof_ideas, and vague_proof_contributions are a type of research use. The distinction between them is the extent of the credit that AI deserves for its contributions. Please read the descriptions carefully and do your best to judge how much credit that AI deserves given the text. If a case is on the borderline between substantial_proof_contributions and vague_proof_contributions, choose vague_proof_contributions. 
>
> USE TAGS — the authors used AI for this paper:
>
> - main_results_generated: AI produced the paper's main results. The proof of the main theorem(s) or the main result(s) is credited to AI directly. If AI contributed or assisted, but isn't credited *in full*, this is not the right tag. For significant contributions that do not fit this tag, see substantial_proof_contributions. Proofs generated by AI but checked, verified, or rewritten by humans count for this tag. 
> - specific_result_generated: a specific, major proof, construction, formula, bound, or
>   counterexample appearing IN the paper is attributed to AI ("the proof of
>   Theorem 3.1 was found by ChatGPT 5.5 Pro"). If AI contributed or assisted, but isn't credited *in full*, this is not the right tag. For significant contributions that do not fit this tag, see substantial_proof_contributions. Proofs generated by AI but checked, verified, or rewritten by humans count for this tag. To count under this tag, the specific result must be a named or numbered statement in the paper (excluding minor statements called things like "fact", "observation", or "remark") or a counterexample to a named or numbered statement. The passage does not need to contain the number itself (it can say something like "the results in this section are due to ChatGPT", for example), but you must be able to deduce a specific numbered result to which it refers. If the result appears in an appendix or is not important enough to contain a number, consider substantial_proof_contributions or vague_proof_contributions. If the specific result is an example or counterexample that is not central to the paper, it is not this tag. (A good test is: if the paper would still exist and be roughly as strong a paper without the example or counterexample, it is not this tag.) If the result is a lemma described as "auxiliary" or "preparatory" or another similar word, consider substantial_proof_contributions or vague_proof_contributions. 
> - statement_generated: AI is credited with producing or suggesting the statement of a conjecture or theorem which forms one of the main results of the paper. If AI is credited with producing or suggesting the statement of a lemma which is not the main result, but which is a named or numbered lemma used in the proof of the main result, count this as substantial_proof_contributions. If AI is credited with producing or suggesting the statement of a lemma which does not appear in the paper or which is a minor result, count this as proof_ideas. 
> - formalization_assist: AI assisted a machine formalization (Lean, Isabelle,
>   Coq) of the paper's own results. The text must attribute the formalization
>   itself to AI — general AI assistance on a paper that also has a formalization
>   does not qualify.
> - substantial_proof_contributions: AI is credited with substantial contributions toward proofs of results in the paper. Examples that fall under this tag include using words like "key", "substantial", "crucial", "essential," or "extensive" to describe the AI contribution; acknowledgements that otherwise emphasize the importance of the AI contribution; or other disclosures of a similar extent and nature as these examples. If a specific idea, result, or technique is credited to AI, and this result, idea, or technique is acknowledged to be a key part of the proof, that counts as this tag. As a general guideline, this tag is intended to be for AI contributions that are at a level clearly sufficient for coauthorship. Acknowledgements that say only that AI "helped", "assisted," or "aided," or other similar words, belong in vague_proof_contributions. Every passage justifying the substantial_proof_contributions label should contain wording that a majority of mathematicians would agree is evidence for a "substantial" contribution. 
> - vague_proof_contributions: AI is credited with contributions toward proofs of results in the paper, with language not strong enough to qualify for substantial_proof_contributions. Examples that fall under this tag are acknowledgements of AI for assisting with proofs; for providing suggestions or ideas such as "the final step of an argument"; for help with proofs or examples; for contributions to proofs; for interaction; for discussions; and similar wording, unless other adjectives or descriptions are used that qualify for substantial_proof_contributions. Vague refers only to the EXTENT of the contribution, not the CONTENT of the contribution. "AI assisted with the proof of Lemma 2.5" counts as vague_proof_contributions. 
> - proof_ideas: AI is credited with specific, minor proof assistance. Examples include acknowledgements that AI helped with brainstorming; testing ideas; testing intuitions; searching for examples or counterexamples; or improving constants. Verifying small cases belongs in math_checking. If AI's research contribution is explicitly limited, e.g. by a sentence saying that the human authors contributed all substantial mathematical content, use this tag. 
> - code_computation: AI assisted with writing code, computation, or simulation. Any code that accompanies or contributes to the research paper, if written or assisted by AI, counts as this tag. To count for this tag, it MUST be stated that AI helped write the code or computation. SageMath, Maple, or other computer algebra systems are not this tag UNLESS AI is explicitly credited with the coding. If AI assisted with writing code for formalizing in Lean, Coq, Isabelle, etc, that is not this tag; that is formalization_assist. 
> - math_checking: AI is credited with verifying proofs, constants, or derivations; with checking proofs and paper content for correctness; and with finding mathematical errors. (Finding typos or grammar errors is writing_polish.)
> - literature_search: AI assisted with finding references, related work, or known results or lemmas that are relevant and/or useful.
> - writing_polish: AI assisted with style, grammar, rephrasing, fluency, LaTeX and formatting
>   help, and/or textual proofreading.
> - draft_text_generated: AI wrote substantial portions of the manuscript's
>   prose, drafting sections wholesale. If AI is credited with drafting *all* material in a section, including the mathematical content, that counts as draft_text_generated *and* as specific_result_generated. 
> - translation: AI assisted with translating the manuscript or sources between languages.
> - figures_media: AI assisted with generating figures, diagrams, artwork, or other media.
> - purpose_unstated: a genuine AI use disclosure that states NO purpose anywhere in the
>   paper ("the authors acknowledge the use of AI tools"). Use it alone among use
>   tags. Generic "this work used AI"-type acknowledgements belong in this tag. 
> - other: a genuine disclosure whose STATED purpose fits no tag above. Quote the
>   stated purpose. Use sparingly.
>
> If a use pattern recurs that none of these captures, you may propose a NEW tag:
> "new:<short_slug>" with a one-line definition in the definition field, plus the
> quote. Prefer a listed tag when one fits; prefer other for a true one-off;
> propose new: only when you see a nameable category.
>
> NON-USE TAGS:
>
> - subject_matter: the paper is about AI, language models, or proof assistants
>   — its topic, not its toolbox. A paper machine-checking or formalizing its own
>   mathematical results in a proof assistant is not subject_matter. This tag
>   co-occurs with use tags when the authors also disclose using AI to produce
>   the paper.
> - no_use_declared: the authors explicitly state they did not use AI. Use this
>   tag ONLY when no use is disclosed anywhere in the paper. A limited-use
>   statement — "we used AI for X; no AI was used beyond this", "no generative AI
>   was used except for language editing" — is NOT this tag: it is the disclosed
>   use tags plus denial_scope: scoped.
> - name_collision: the matched term is a person's name or unrelated proper noun
>   (Claude Shannon, Jean-Claude, Coquand, the Gemini constellation, an
>   institution name like "Technical University of the Shannon"), or mathematical
>   notation that spells an AI term (a \mathrm{AI} macro for automorphic
>   induction). Valid alongside genuine disclosure tags on the same paper.
> - passing_mention: an AI term appears (citation, related work, aside) but no
>   use of it in producing this paper is disclosed.
>
> WORKED EXAMPLES. Real sentences from arXiv papers, each with its tag and the
> reason. The pairs are deliberately close; the reason is the rule, not the wording.
>
> - "The proofs in this paper are due to ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Pro, and the paper is merely an exposition of its output." → main_results_generated. All the mathematics is credited to AI.
> - "Many of the key ideas of the proofs for the main theorems were obtained from iterated guided conversations with ChatGPT." → substantial_proof_contributions. Key ideas, but the proofs are not credited to AI in full.
> - "ChatGPT wrote a version of the exponential martingale argument that became Lemma 3.2." → specific_result_generated. A numbered statement whose proof is credited to AI.
> - "Claude proposed a clearer and substantially streamlined proof of the path-existence results in Appendix B, replacing an earlier incomplete argument." → substantial_proof_contributions. Attributed proof, but in an appendix, so not the strict tag.
> - "The formulation and proof strategy of Lemma 2.1 were substantially inspired by suggestions generated through interactions with this AI tool." → substantial_proof_contributions. A numbered lemma, but "inspired by suggestions" is not credit in full.
> - "The preliminary AI-assisted exploration led to Conjecture 1.1 and suggested a candidate counterexample." → statement_generated. AI produced the statement of a main result.
> - "ChatGPT contributed significantly to the proof of Theorem 1.2, which involved a lot of joint brainstorming, and to improving the bounds." → substantial_proof_contributions. "Significantly" qualifies the contribution to a named result.
> - "The final idea of the decomposition used in the proof of Theorem 3 is due to ChatGPT." → substantial_proof_contributions. A specific, named idea in a specific proof.
> - "The main result was obtained with the assistance of GPT-5.6 Sol." → vague_proof_contributions. Assistance, with neither the activity nor the object specified.
> - "GPT-5.6 Sol was used to assist in the development of proof techniques appearing in Section 3." → vague_proof_contributions. Same: assistance without a named activity or object.
> - "We used an AI assistant (ChatGPT, model: GPT-5.2 Pro) in the brainstorming process (only) for Section 4." → proof_ideas. Brainstorming is a named minor activity, and "(only)" explicitly limits the contribution.
> - "GPT-5.6 Sol was used to quickly test intuitions, to help improve the constant coefficients, and to check for typos." → proof_ideas and writing_polish. Named minor activities, one tag each.
> - "Claude was used to write the first four paragraphs of the introduction, parts of the preliminaries, and the proof of Lemma 3 and Theorem 5." → draft_text_generated and specific_result_generated. Prose is drafting; the numbered proofs are the strict tag.
> - "Using the Z3 Theorem Prover we have obtained the integral sum labellings of the 4D-cube." → no use tag (passing_mention if a term matched here). A solver, computer algebra system, or search program is conventional software, not AI, unless the paper calls it an AI system or credits an AI with writing or running it.
> - "The author thanks Stijn Cambie, ChatGPT, and Quanyu Tang for discussion (in alphabetical order)." → vague_proof_contributions. Playful framing, real use, nothing specified.
>
> BORDERLINE FLAGS: some boundaries are genuinely fuzzy. When a tag sits on one
> of these, set its borderline field to the boundary's name rather than guessing
> confidently:
> - specific_result_generated / substantial_proof_contributions — is the result credited to AI in full, or did AI contribute to it?
> - substantial_proof_contributions / proof_ideas — does the contribution rise to the level of coauthorship?
> - statement_generated / proof_ideas — is the AI-suggested statement a main result of the paper, or a lemma or minor result?
> - draft_text_generated / specific_result_generated — did AI draft only the prose, or the mathematical content as well?
> - math_checking / writing_polish
> - formalization_assist scope
> - no_use_declared / scoped denial
> - subject_matter / authors' own use
>
> FIELDS beside the tags:
>
> - tools_named: every AI tool or proof assistant the paper credits, one entry per
>   distinct tool, each with two parts. name: the tool VERBATIM as the paper spells
>   it, version strings included ("ChatGPT 5.5 Pro", not "ChatGPT"). quote: the
>   verbatim sentence in which the paper credits that tool — the receipt, exactly as
>   for tags. Include specialized mathematics AI systems (Aristotle, AxiomMath,
>   FunSearch, TxGraffiti) and proof assistants (Lean, Isabelle) — do NOT decide
>   whose product a tool is or whether it "counts" as AI; extraction is your job,
>   vendor mapping is not. When the paper names only the maker and no product ("the
>   authors used OpenAI models", "an internal model at Anthropic", "Google's AI
>   tools"), record that phrase as the name — the maker is the tool's only identity
>   in the text. A maker thanked only for funding, compute, or access ("we thank
>   OpenAI for API credits") is not a tool unless a product is also credited.
> - denial_scope: blanket for "no AI was used in this work"; scoped for a denial
>   that sits alongside disclosed uses ("no AI was used for the mathematical
>   content"); none otherwise.
> - venue: where the primary disclosure lives — a dedicated "Declaration of
>   generative AI..." section is declaration_section; otherwise acknowledgments,
>   footnote, or body. none if no disclosure.
> - comment_only: true only when NO disclosure quote comes from the rendered
>   stream — everything sits in TeX comments.
> - For a paper with no disclosure at all: is_disclosure false, the applicable
>   non-use tags, venue none, comment_only false.
>
> CONFIDENCE, on every tag: how sure you are that this tag is the right one for
> its quote. high: the quoted text states the use plainly and the tag follows
> directly from it. medium: the tag rests on interpreting a hedged, general, or
> unusually worded statement, or the quote could defensibly support a neighbouring
> tag. low: a careful reader could reasonably assign a different tag to this
> quote, or none. A tag with a borderline flag is at most medium.
>
> REFERENCED RESULTS, on every tag: when the quoted passage credits AI with results
> that live elsewhere in the paper — "the results in Section 3", "the proofs in the
> appendix", "the lemmas of Part II" — record what it points at. referenced_results
> lists the labels as the paper names them ("Theorem 3.1", "Lemma 3.4", "Appendix
> B"); for a section or appendix, list the numbered statements it contains. This is
> where you write down the specific numbered result you deduced for
> specific_result_generated. referenced_quote is a VERBATIM span from the referenced
> location, normally the statement of the first result listed, so the target can be
> checked. Leave both empty when the quote itself names the result or points at
> nothing.

## 3. The output schema (strict JSON, enforced by the API)

| Field | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `is_disclosure` | bool | any use tag with a rendered-stream quote |
| `tags[]` | array | each: `tag` (closed enum + `new:` pattern), `quote` (verbatim), `stream` (rendered/comment), `definition` (new: only), `borderline` (named boundary or empty), `confidence` (high / medium / low, per tag, defined in the prompt), `referenced_results` (labels the passage points at, e.g. "Theorem 3.1"; empty when the quote names the result), `referenced_quote` (verbatim span from the referenced location, or empty) |
| `tools_named[]` | array | each: `name` (verbatim tool name with version), `quote` (the verbatim sentence crediting it — every vendor row has a receipt) |
| `denial_scope` | none / blanket / scoped | |
| `venue` | declaration_section / acknowledgments / footnote / body / none | |
| `comment_only` | bool | disclosure exists only in TeX comments |

The closed enum means the model cannot invent tag names outside `new:`; the
`quote` requirement is backstopped mechanically — every returned quote is
substring-verified against the paper (unicode-normalized, ellipsis-spliced quotes
verified fragment by fragment), and a tag whose quote does not verify is
auto-flagged, never silently published.

### The second instruction channel: schema descriptions

The strict schema carries a per-field `description` the model receives as part of
the output contract — these sentences are model-visible instructions too, and are
reviewed here alongside the prompt:

- `tags[].quote` — "Verbatim span from the paper that supports this tag.
  Mandatory — no quote, no tag. Copy exact characters; never paraphrase or join
  spans with ellipses."
- `tags[].borderline` — "The named boundary this tag sits on, when a defensible
  reading goes either way. Empty when the tag is clear."
- `tags[].definition` — "ONLY for new:<slug> tags: one-line definition of the
  proposed category. Empty otherwise."
- `tools_named[].name` — "The tool verbatim as the paper spells it, version string
  included. One entry per distinct tool."
- `tools_named[].quote` — "Verbatim sentence in which the paper credits this tool.
  Mandatory — no quote, no tool."
- `denial_scope` — "blanket: no AI used at all. scoped: a denial alongside
  disclosed uses."
- `venue` — "Where the primary disclosure lives. none if no disclosure."
- `comment_only` — "True iff every disclosure quote comes from TeX comments —
  nothing reaches the rendered paper."
- `tags[].confidence` — "How sure you are this tag is right for its quote: high
  when the text states the use plainly; medium when the tag rests on
  interpretation or the quote could support a neighbouring tag; low when a careful
  reader could reasonably assign a different tag, or none. Borderline-flagged tags
  are at most medium."
- `tags[].referenced_results` — "Labels of the results the quoted passage points at
  elsewhere in the paper, as the paper names them (e.g. 'Theorem 3.1', 'Appendix
  B'). For a section or appendix, the numbered statements it contains. Empty when the
  quote itself names the result or points at nothing."
- `tags[].referenced_quote` — "Verbatim span from the referenced location, normally
  the statement of the first result listed in referenced_results. Empty when
  referenced_results is empty."
- `is_disclosure` — "True iff the authors disclose their own use of AI tools in producing THIS paper (any use tag with a
  rendered-stream quote)."

## 3a. Published rollup 

Fine tags roll up to the eight buckets below. Multi-label, not a partition; a paper
can sit in several. `purpose_unstated` is used alone among use tags, which is what
keeps "non-specific acknowledgement" disjoint from the rest.

Which buckets are *published* is decided downstream, by the two-reader agreement
threshold, not here. In the current snapshot six of the eight clear it: substantial
research, formalization, research assistance, code & computation, literature search and
writing. Tags rolling into the other two carry an empty `bucket` in
`math_disclosures.csv`, so a paper can count in the disclosure rate and sit in no
bucket, and the buckets do not sum to the rate.

| Bucket | Fine tags |
| --- | --- |
| Substantial research | `main_results_generated`, `specific_result_generated`, `substantial_proof_contributions`, `statement_generated` |
| Formalization | `formalization_assist` |
| Research assistance | `proof_ideas`, `vague_proof_contributions`, `math_checking` |
| Code & computation | `code_computation` |
| Literature search | `literature_search` |
| Writing | `writing_polish`, `draft_text_generated`, `translation` |
| Non-specific acknowledgement | `purpose_unstated` |
| Other | `figures_media`, `other` |

---

## 3b. Vendor is downstream, on purpose

The model never judges vendors. `ai_ack_vendor` is computed after classification, by
an ordered regex table mapping the verbatim `tools_named` strings to vendor buckets,
with a not-a-vendor guard (Lean, mathlib, Grammarly…) and a generic-tool guard ("an
LLM" → `unnamed`). Adding a vendor is a table edit and a republish, never a
re-classification. The receipts are published in `math_tools.csv`, one row per credited
tool with the sentence crediting it, so any mapping here can be checked or redone.
